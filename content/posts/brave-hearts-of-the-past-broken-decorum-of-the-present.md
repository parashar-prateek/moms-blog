As India marks 79 cherished years of freedom, the nation proudly celebrates its 80th Independence Day on 15.8.2026. August 15 is not just a date of celebration; it is a reminder of the blood, sweat and tears of our ancestors. Countless brave freedom fighters willingly gave up their youth, their comfort, and their lives so that we could breathe in a free India.

India's independence is not just the legacy of a few elite families or celebrated individuals. It is a collective achievement. Every citizen contributed according to their ability and ordinary masses played a vital part in breaking the chains of colonial rule. On this historic occasion, let us remember India's youngest martyrs who fell at the hands of British rulers.

Two extraordinary 12-year-olds stand out as symbols of ultimate bravery.

To honour the role of women in our freedom struggle, we first remember India's youngest female martyr:

1. TILESWARI BARUA FROM ASSAM
2. BAJI ROUT FROM ODISHA

---

TILESWARI BARUA:- Tileswari Barua was the eldest daughter of Shri Bhabakanta Barua, a poor marginal farmer, and Smt. Jonki Devi Barua. Her family lived on the outskirts of Dhekiajuli in Assam.

Born in 1930, she was just 12 years old when she joined the MRITYU BAHINI (DEATH SQUAD) to march toward a BRITISH COLONIAL POLICE STATION with a single goal - to hoist the Indian tricolour. During the march, Tileswari was positioned right behind the squad leader, Late Sh. Monbor Nath. When Nath climbed the roof and was gunned down, Tileswari stepped into the line of fire without fear. She was holding a small tricolour flag in her hand. She courageously shouted VANDE MATARAM, VANDE MATARAM before being shot and killed during the open police firing on 20.09.1942. Today she is rightfully recognised as India's youngest female martyr.

---

BAJI ROUT:- Baji Rout, the brave boat boy, was the son of Shri Hari Rout and Rania Devi. Sh. Hari Rout was a boatman who worked on the Brahmani River and passed away during Baji's early childhood. His mother, Rania Devi, raised Baji alone, doing small jobs like de-husking rice to support the family.

Baji Rout, born on 05.10.1926 in the Dhenkanal district of ODISHA, is recognised as the youngest male martyr in the history of India's freedom struggle.

Just at the age of 12 years, Baji became an active member of the VANAR SENA (the children's wing of the Revolutionary Prajamandal Movement).

On the rainy night of 11.10.1938, he volunteered to keep watch at the NILAKANTHAPUR GHAT on the Brahmani River.

When British police troops arrived and ordered Baji to ferry them across the river to hunt down fleeing freedom fighters, the young boy flatly refused. Despite brutal threats, Baji stood firm to protect his country's freedom fighters who were all hiding across the river. Furious at his confidence and defiance, a British policeman shot him in the head, killing him on the spot.

---

These children did not live to see free India, but their courage proved that patriotism knows no age. The ultimate sacrifice of Tileswari Barua and Baji Rout serves as a profound reminder that the youth have always been the heartbeat of India. However, as we recently witnessed, the 50-day protest at Jantar Mantar (from 6th June to 25th July 2026), which erupted over systemic national examination failure, exposed a darker facet of youth activism, sparking a nationwide debate regarding the boundaries of free speech and social decorum.

India is a vibrant democracy where citizens have the right to protest when their grievances are not heard at appropriate forums, but this liberty does not authorise anyone to cross ethical limits. Using public podiums to hurl abusive language or derogatory remarks deeply erodes the moral fabric and cultural values of our society. True patriotism is not found in loud, disrespectful dissent or aggressive speeches, but it lives in the unbreakable, dignified courage shown by our youngest historical heroes.

---

Character building begins at home; families must guide the next generation to carry itself with dignity in public places. As tomorrow's custodians of this great nation, today's youth must use their energies on actions that spread a positive message to society. By elevating their conduct and intellect to a level that commands respect, they can ensure that the entire nation stands proud of its future leaders.

True leadership cannot be demanded through noise and hooliganism; it must be earned through dignified, purposeful action:

"KHUDI KO KAR BULAND ITNA KE HAR TAQDEER SE PEHLE KHUDA BANDE SE KHUD POOCHE BATA TERI RAZA KYA HAI"

For a brighter future, every generation must work together as one team, acknowledging that everyone has something valuable to offer.

Let us bow down to our ancestors with deep gratitude for the freedom we enjoy today, and also remind our youth one more time that the future is in their hands:

"YEH DESH HAI TUMHAARA, NETA TUM HI HO KAL KE"

---

We inherit a legacy of courage. TOGETHER WE RISE, TOGETHER WE LEAD. Let every generation walk hand-in-hand to reclaim the true spirit of India.
