In the 12th century, a legendary love story of SASSI AND PUNNU bloomed that defied all boundaries.

The tragic romance of SASSI AND PUNNU originated in Sindhi and Balochistan folklore. However, it gained immense popularity in Punjab when famous sufi poets, like Hashim Shah, rewrote it as a QISSA OF SASSI AND PUNNU. Today, this cherished tale remains an integral important part of both Punjabi and Sindhi literature.

---

SASSI AND PUNNU:- Punnu was a wealthy prince from the Baloch dynasty of Makran and Sassi was a royal princess born in the city of Bhambore in Sindh. Upon her birth, astrologers predicted she would bring dishonour to the family, her frightened parents oder her to be placed in a wooden box and cast her into the Indus River. She was saved and raised by a humble washerman (dhobi).

Sassi fell deeply in love with Punnu just by looking at his portrait. To attract his trade caravan, she built a beautiful, fragrant garden and when his trade caravan arrived near the garden, sweet scent drew Punnu in, and the moment they met, they fell in love. Punnu refused to return and decided to marry her, defying his royal family's objections to marrying a washerman's daughter, Punnu chose to stay in BHAMBORE and marry her against his family's wishes.

On their wedding night, Punnu's brothers heavily drugged him, tied his unconscious body to a camel, and dragged him back to his homeland.

Sassi woke up to an empty bed. Instead of breaking down, her immense willpower drove her to look for her husband. She walked for days until she reached Singher, a village in Lasbela, situated geographically between Bhambore and Makran, this is the border area of Sind and Balochistan.

Sassi was dehydrated and physically exhausted from trekking the harsh desert barefoot. She encountered a shepherd who attempted to assault her, Sassi prayed to God to protect her honour, the ground split open and swallowed her, leaving only a corner of her dupatta visible above the sand.

When Punnu gained his consciousness, he realised he had been separated from his wife Sassi, he immediately rushed to BHAMBORE and on his way, the shepherd narrated the whole story to Punnu and showed him the spot where earth swallowed Sassi. He wept in grief, prayed to rejoin her, and the earth opened once more to bury them together forever. The repentant shepherd who witnessed the tragedy built their joint resting place exactly on the spot where earth swallowed both of them.

It remains a renowned Sufi Shrine and a monument of eternal devotion where devotees and Sufi folk singers from Sindh, Balochistan, and Punjab gather annually at the Shrine to pay their respect and celebrate their love story.

We still sing about them at weddings because Sassi represents incredible female resilience, dignity, and willpower. Their story serves as a beautiful reminder to newly wed couples that true love is brave, selfless, and lasting.

Today technology and social media have completely changed how we view love, relationship and marriage. Dating apps make finding a partner very easy. However, when choices seem endless, commitment losses its value, and people often choose to replace their partners rather than work together through difficult times.

These days, we increasingly witness horrifying incidents where the sacred definition of marriage/relationships is reduced to greed, obsession and self-interest. Barely a week passes without a headline of a trusted partner turning into a killer - honeymoons that end in tragedy, live-in relationships that end in unspeakable brutality, a spouse plotting against the very person they had vowed to love. These heartbreaking incidents prove how easily greed can wipe out loyalty and compassion. They show that when selfish interest takes over, the person who promises to love you can become the one who destroys you.

---

Such incidents reveal the difference between devotional love of Sassi Punnu and the modern days love. The story of Sassi and punnu reminds us of what true love looks like "A BOND WHERE TWO PEOPLE ARE WILLING TO LOOSE EVERYTHING JUST TO BE TOGETHER". The tragic contrast with modern love comes down to one simple shift:

SASSI PUNNU ASKED "WHAT CAN I GIVE UP FOR YOU" WHEREAS TODAY'S LOVE OFTEN ASKS, "WHAT CAN I GET OUT OF YOU".

Love used to be a sacrifice; today, it is often a transaction. True love is not business deal where you calculate your gains, but a selfless promise to give your best without keeping any score.
